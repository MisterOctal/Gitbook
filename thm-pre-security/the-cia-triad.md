### Task 1: Introduction

The CIA triad is the absolute baseline of information security. It stands for Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability. Every security policy or technical control you implement is ultimately trying to protect one or more of these three pillars. It is a simple concept but it forms the foundation for protecting any network.

### Task 2: Understanding the CIA Triad

Security does not simply mean stopping attacks. It means ensuring specific conditions for digital data. Confidentiality keeps data secret from unauthorized people. Integrity ensures data is not maliciously or accidentally altered. Availability ensures the data and systems are accessible when authorized users need them. Pretty much everything you encounter during your journey through cybersecurity will revolve around defending or attacking these three pillars.

An example of the entire CIA triad being broken is when data is seen, changed, then not delivered to the recipient. This scenario violates the entire triad.

Question: Which pillar of the CIA focuses on preventing unauthorized modification of data?

> Answer: Integrity

Question: Which pillar of the CIA focuses on preventing unauthorized access to data?

> Answer: Confidentiality

Question: Which CIA pillar ensures data is available to users when needed?

> Answer: Availability

Question: Which CIA pillar gets impacted if the data becomes untrustworthy?

> Answer: Integrity

Question: What is the term used collectively for all these pillars?

> Answer: CIA Triad

### Task 3: The Security Mindset

The CIA triad is more than just definitions. It is a mindset that professionals use to evaluate incidents and build secure systems. When a security incident occurs, assessing it against these three pillars helps determine the exact impact and the appropriate response. For example, knowing whether an attacker just viewed sensitive records or actively altered them completely changes how you respond to the breach.
